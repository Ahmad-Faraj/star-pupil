# Star Pupil

Every AI product wants to teach you. This one asks you to do the teaching.

Star Pupil gives you a student. It knows nothing about your topic, and it will believe exactly what you tell it, including the things you explain badly.

Before you say a word, the examiner draws the subject as a map, ten or so concepts with the prerequisites between them, and seals an exam paper against it. You never get to see that map. What you see is the frontier: the handful of concepts you could teach next, and nothing behind them. Teach one and the next ones surface. Handing you the full syllabus would turn the lesson into a checklist, and then the exam is only measuring how well you worked through a list.

Explain something clearly and a concept turns gold. Explain something sloppily and you get to watch a misconception take root in real time, with your own sentence quoted as the source, and an edge showing you which later concept it went on to poison.

You can argue with any of it. Tell Pip he never heard you say that and he reads your own sentence back, word for word, and keeps his note. He only backs down when the quote genuinely does not say what he wrote down, and when he does, the report card admits the ledger was edited. A record you can overturn by insisting would not be worth showing you.

Then comes the exam, and the map opens up. The whole subject, including everything you never reached, which is the first time you see it. Your pupil sits the paper alone and can only answer from what you taught. Every mark it loses traces back to a specific moment in your lesson, and if a wrong belief quietly built on top of an earlier one, the trace follows the chain back to the root, not just the nearest mistake. Ask to see his working and the map draws the route he actually took, numbered, ending on the concept the question was set on. Next to the sentence that cost you the mark is the one that would not have. The report card it brings home is not grading the student. It also splits into two numbers: how much of the subject you actually covered, and how much of what you covered was right, because a confident 6/6 on half the topic is a different lesson than a shaky 6/6 on all of it.

The idea is old and good: the protege effect. Teaching something is the fastest way to find out you never understood it. Star Pupil just makes the evidence impossible to argue with. If you'd rather see the payoff before teaching your own lesson, skip straight to a finished report card from the enroll screen.

Built during a hackathon season, July 2026. Engine first, then the classroom.
